### 1.3 The technology stack (this is the story spine)

The BRAND-DNA document defines four layers. They map cleanly onto a scroll narrative:

**Layer 01 — YUBASE.** Premium Group III base oil, not an additive. It is the foundation.
Refined via hydrocracking/hydroisomerisation to strip out the impurities (sulphur, aromatics,
wax) that make conventional Group I/II oils oxidise, thicken and sludge up.
*Principle: "Performance begins with the base."*

**Layer 02 — VHVI Technology.** Very High Viscosity Index. The property that YUBASE delivers.
A high VI means the oil's thickness changes **less** as temperature swings. This is the single
most South-Africa-relevant technical property on the whole page — see §3.

*Principle: "Stable under change."*

**Layer 03 — Additive formulation.** Detergents, dispersants, anti-oxidants, anti-wear agents,
friction modifiers, pour-point depressants, anti-foam, VI improvers.
*Principle: "Base oil builds the foundation. Additives tune the function."*

**Layer 04 — Lubrication functions.** Reduce friction and wear · cool · clean · protect against
rust and corrosion · seal · disperse mechanical load.

### 1.4 Lubrication states (the "Protection Layer" signature device)

- **Fluid-film** — surfaces fully separated by oil. The ideal state.
- **Boundary** — surfaces nearly touching; the additive film is all that stands between them.
  This is cold start, and it is where most engine wear actually happens.
- **Extreme-pressure** — very high load; EP additives chemically bond to the metal.

The hero and the technology chapter should visualise the transition between these states. That
is the answer to BRAND-DNA's master visual question: *what is happening inside?*

## 3. South African market lens

The BRAND-DNA localisation list, converted into the actual engineering argument for each:

| SA condition | What it does to oil | The ZIC answer |
|---|---|---|
| Johannesburg stop-start traffic | Repeated heat cycling; oil never reaches steady state; fuel and moisture dilution | Oxidation stability, detergency |
| Durban summer heat + humidity | Oil thins at temperature; film strength drops | High VI, film strength at high temp |
| Highveld winter starts (frost, ~1,750 m altitude) | Cold oil is thick; slow to reach the top end; boundary-lubrication wear | Low-temperature fluidity, pour point |
| N1/N3 long-haul highway | Sustained high load and temperature for hours | Oxidation stability, drain-interval durability |
| Mountain passes (Van Reenen's, Du Toitskloof) | Sustained high load at low road speed, poor airflow | Film strength under load |
| Fleet and last-mile delivery duty | Short trips, high idle, soot loading in diesels | Soot control, dispersancy |
| Bakkie and LDV dominance | CRDI diesel, turbo, high torque, often loaded | Diesel-specific soot and wear packages |
| Ageing mixed vehicle parc | Worn clearances, oil consumption, leaks | Higher-viscosity high-mileage grades |
| Korean vehicle ownership | Hyundai/Kia gearbox fluids are specification-critical | ATF SP-III / SP-IV, CVTF, DCTF |

**The strategic insight.** Viscosity index is not a spec-sheet curiosity in South Africa — it is
the whole product argument. A vehicle can see −3 °C on a Highveld winter morning and a 105 °C
sump on the N3 to Durban the same week. VHVI is the property that keeps the oil doing its job at
both ends. Build the technology chapter on that.

**The second strategic insight.** Parts-Mall Africa's customer base is disproportionately
Hyundai/Kia. For those vehicles, **ATF SP-III and SP-IV are not optional cross-references** —
using the wrong ATF damages the transmission. ZIC makes both. This is a genuine differentiator
that a generic oil brand cannot claim, and it deserves its own section.

## 5. Copy constraints carried from BRAND-DNA

Non-negotiable, and worth restating because they are the things most easily got wrong:

1. **Specification before recommendation.** Always: find the manufacturer's required spec first,
   then match the ZIC product. Never recommend by vehicle age, appearance or driving style.
2. **Claims are product-specific.** X9's MB approval is X9's. It does not transfer to X7 or X5.
3. **The claim verbs are not synonyms.** *Approved by* means OEM-certified. *Meets or exceeds*
   is a self-declaration. Do not upgrade a verb for punchier copy.
4. **Never override OEM service intervals** when talking about extended drain.
5. **No quantified fuel-economy figures** unless the specific product has the test data.
6. **No absolute lifespan guarantees.**
7. **Red is active, never decorative.** Flow paths, CTAs, active states, proof points. Never
   flame effects or racing aggression.
8. **No em dashes in copy.** BRAND-DNA and the design standard agree on this.
